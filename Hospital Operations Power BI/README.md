# Hospital Operations & Quality Analytics
## Project Overview
The Hospital Operations & Quality Analytics dashboard provides hospital leaders with a clear, interactive view of operational performance, financial results, and quality of care. Built in Power BI, it consolidates key metrics and trends to support informed decision-making.

Core indicators show 500 total encounters, $2.70 million in billed revenue, an average patient satisfaction score of 3.63, an average length of stay of 7.63 days, and a low readmission rate of 0.17%.

Key findings reveal seasonal variation in monthly encounters, with lower activity early in the year and stronger volumes mid-year and at year-end. Orthopedics accounts for the highest number of encounters, followed by Cardiology, Oncology, Neurology, and General Surgery. Average treatment costs remain consistent across the four hospitals. Medicare represents the largest share of encounters, with a notable portion of uninsured patients. Encounters are distributed across both genders and all age groups, with higher volumes in middle and older age ranges.

These insights highlight stable overall performance alongside clear opportunities to align resources with demand, focus on high-volume service lines, and address payer-mix implications. The dashboard serves as a practical tool for monitoring operations and driving continuous improvement in hospital performance and quality of care.

## Business Problem
Management needs a clear and interactive way to monitor hospital operations and identify areas that require attention.

The dashboard was designed to answer the following questions:
1.  What is driving the monthly fluctuations in encounter volume, and how can staffing or capacity be adjusted to match peak periods?
2.  Why does Orthopedics generate significantly more encounters than other medical conditions, and is this aligned with strategic priorities?
3.  How does encounter volume differ across hospitals, and are certain facilities under- or over-utilized?
4.  Why are average treatment costs relatively consistent across hospitals and does this indicate standardized care pathways or limited cost differentiation?
5.  How does the high proportion of Medicare and uninsured patients impact overall revenue and collection rates?
6.  Which medical conditions or hospitals contribute most to the revenue?
7.  How does the distribution of encounters by age and gender influence resource planning and care pathway design?
8.  Are certain age groups or genders over-represented in high-volume conditions such as Orthopedics or Cardiology?
9.  Does the payer mix (especially the uninsured segment) vary significantly by hospital or medical condition?
10.  Which combination of filters (hospital, condition, admission type, age, or gender) reveals the greatest opportunities for operational or quality improvement?
11.  How can the organization leverage the dominance of Orthopedics and Cardiology to improve overall efficiency and patient outcomes?
12.  What actions should be prioritized to maintain the low readmission rate while addressing seasonal demand and payer-mix challenges?
